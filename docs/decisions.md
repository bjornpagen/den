# Decisions

## Settled by the owner, 2026-11-11

- **Name:** den.
- **The algebra is Effect's.** den implements an engine for `effect/workflow` as Effect's newest release ships it.
  No new computation language. A shortfall of the algebra is reported in the review and pauses the work.
- **Glue Effect's upstream parts.** When in doubt, den uses Effect's upstream code and glues the parts together
  (`effect/cluster`, `effect/persistence`, `effect/sql`, `effect/rpc`, Cron, Schedule, RateLimiter and the rest)
  rather than reinventing them.
- **grizzle is the only store; PostgreSQL 19 only.** grizzle beats `effect/sql` wherever an Effect part reaches for
  SQL, unless there is no reason to: grizzle on PostgreSQL is a real improvement over a generic SQL driver. An upstream
  part built on `effect/sql` is glued in through a grizzle-backed implementation of its interface.
- **Vercel Queues, through a transactional outbox**, wake the functions that run the work.
- **First use:** a reimplementation of edullm's queueing code inside den. Nothing is backported into edullm.

## Owner direction, 2026-11-11, to be designed

- **The bet: grizzle holds the computation graph as a graph.** Steps are immutable facts in typed relations, their
  edges are laws PostgreSQL checks, and no payload or result is a JSON blob. No document-store or column-store hacks.
- **The full graph, no hedging (owner, 2026-11-11).** den records the whole computation graph: every step and every
  edge between steps, including data edges from a result to the steps it feeds, joins with several parents, fan-in,
  and edges discovered while running. Effect's algebra records only a tree of executions and their named results;
  where it is partial, den goes past it. Every API, Effect's included, is judged against this target. No local
  maximums.
- **No fire-and-forget work (owner, 2026-11-11).** Every piece of work started is joined by a scope that waits for
  it; work nobody joins is unrepresentable. Joining children is part of the core.
- **den's own core if Effect's is not good enough (owner, 2026-11-11).** den may build its own tight, algebraic core,
  as grizzle did for the relational model: "it's the same move." Effect stays the language a step's body is written
  in.
- **The graph is fully declared (owner, 2026-11-11).** Every step kind, scope kind and edge kind is declared with
  its types before anything runs; a result decides only which declared edges are taken and how often.
- **den builds on grizzle's `main` branch, pinned (owner, 2026-11-14):** "let's move den to point to grizzle main".
  `vendor/grizzle` is a commit of github.com/bjornpagen/grizzle's `main`, into which `live` merged, for its live reads,
  HTTP routes, OpenAPI documents, typed client and `CallError`.
- **Fully porous (owner, 2026-11-11).** den plugs into any grizzle schema and never takes over the installation. Its
  declarations lower and are erased into the app's own unified schema, with no installation, root, login or schema
  of den's own, so the app's own grizzle code sits beside den's, reads and joins its relations, and is written by its
  steps, with nothing making that colocation hard. The floor: den's relations in a named schema of their own,
  generated by the same `grizzle generate` run, in the same installation and migrations chain as the app's schema.
  The ceiling: erased into the app's own schema.
- **The workloads den serves (owner, 2026-11-11):** everything edullm runs today, and nothing invented beside it.
  W1 Timeback's replica copy, W2 AlphaTest, W3 QTI, W4 the Gold refresh, W5 the tutor's takedown timers, W6 the
  Timeback reports, W7 enrollment's reconciliation, W8 the Silver seed. A scheduler feature no one of these needs is
  not built. The owner's order of 2026-11-18 adds the QTI app's assignment engine (below).
- **A step's reads are declared, and recorded as its input (owner, 2026-11-11).** A step kind declares the reads it
  needs; they are taken before its body runs and recorded with its result, so every input a step saw is a fact of the
  graph. A read that depends on what a body computed becomes its own step, joined by an edge.
- **Bounds are exact seats (settled by the evidence, 2026-11-11).** A resource's capacity is its seats, as rows, each
  count a limit with evidence (W1's replica: 8, the operator's hold on a shared bastion); a step holds a seat while it
  runs, and a scope holds one for its life, which is exclusivity (one live run per relation, per collection, per
  request kind, one Gold build). No two holders share a seat key, so a bound is never exceeded. AlphaTest's adaptive
  window bounds requests inside a step, not steps; no workload needs a database or discovered resource. Taking and
  giving back seats certifies SERIALIZABLE at `a226cbac`; grizzle's LEASE moves the same relations to READ COMMITTED.
- **A pause handle has no seats (settled by metis's switch, 2026-11-21).** metis's cutover pauses every Timeback write
  and every Anthropic call, and no limit of either bounds how many run at once, so a step names each by
  `pauseHandle(name)` in its `uses`, beside its resources: a target of its flow's pauses (`<P>Pausable`, with the
  resources and the root kinds), held by a pause as a resource is, that takes nothing when the step runs. It is a kind
  of its own, not a resource: every seat fact (the `<P>Resource` roster, `<P>Seats`, `<P>Hold`, `<P>Live`,
  `<P>SeatBelowCount`) ranges over resources alone, so no seat count, seat row or live hold can name a handle.
  - Rejected: a resource with an invented seat count, a number no provider limit defends (metis carried 151 and 10);
    a resource whose seats may be "unbounded", an arm every seat fact would have to filter out again; and an optional
    or sentinel count.
- **A step's writes into the app's relations run under the app's own role (owner, 2026-11-11).** A flow names the
  app role that writes its steps' app relations, and den adds its commit programs to that role; den never creates a
  writer of the app's tables, and any certification effect shows in the app's own role's `grizzle generate` report.
- **Settled by the evidence, from the model's ambiguities (2026-11-11):**
  - Ringing: the committing function continues with its own first spawn and rings the rest; the relay rings what it
    did not and what anyone else's commit made eligible. Both ring the same step harmlessly, since the claim decides.
  - Exclusivity and windows belong to root kinds only: edullm's are all per run (one live run per relation, per
    collection, one Gold build; the 07:00 to 13:00Z window). A root whose exclusivity seat is held is not admitted and
    is recorded held, as edullm's "that relation skips the night"; no child scope waits on a seat, so none deadlocks.
  - A step's declared reads are recorded with its result, in the commit; a step that dies after reading reads again.
  - A reclaim (a holder that died) counts as an attempt on the step's ladder; a wait (a transient refusal) does not.
  - Retired keys are remembered by grizzle's RETIRE; den keeps no record of its own.
  - The Gold refresh (W4) has no window: its trigger is 13:18Z or an operator's press.
- **The API is decided on technical merit.** Whether den keeps Effect's `Workflow`/`Activity` API as it is or offers
  a graph of steps with no workflow above them is decided by what is great about Effect's API and whether den can
  measurably do better.
- **Every step is an immutable fact.** grizzle's insert-only relations make each step's record a ledger entry, which
  systems that keep step state in their own store apart from the user's data cannot do. There is no separate kind
  of record for workflows and for steps: the whole computation is one graph of facts.
- **As atomic as possible, with as little coordination as possible.** Where a write only adds new facts under their
  keys, no further coordination is needed; coordination happens only where it cannot be avoided.

## Owner direction, 2026-11-18: the assignment engine

- **den serves the assignment engine (owner, 2026-11-18).** Bjorn: "spawn a subagent to focus on the den fix, commit
  and push up. also den needs to be repinned to the very latest green grizzle", and "if there are upstream bugs in den
  that we should fix to unlock our dream workflow, we should do it." This extends the eight workloads by one: the QTI
  app's assignment engine (edullm's `.agents/skills/bjorns-assignment-engine`). Per child, at decision time, one den
  flow reads the child's ledger rows and the content nearest by pgvector, calls Gemini 4 Hydrogen through Vercel's AI
  Gateway for a structured decision, which becomes the step's recorded output exactly once, and writes the app's
  Decision, Stage and ItemSession rows in the same commit; at publish time a chain of pages embeds the content with
  gemini-embedding-2. A feature the engine needs is built; nothing beside it is.
- **A step's reads are one grizzle query, named, taken in one statement, and recorded once per root (settled by the
  engine's need, 2026-11-18, under Bjorn's order that a gap the engine needs is fixed upstream, never worked around;
  restated by AlphaTest's, 2026-11-20).** A step declares `reads(query, records, params)`: `query` is a function of
  the flow's app role to one grizzle query, `query(W).reads({ name: query, ... })`, a record of named queries, any a
  grizzle query states (equalities, a range or a set of values, an exact order by a computed distance and a `take`,
  `approximateNearest` from a parameter's vector or a stored row's, a hybrid of searches); `params` takes its
  parameters, `<name>.<parameter>`, from the step's input; and `records` gives each name's record, a flat struct of
  columns whose type holds that query's rows. den prints `deferrable(<flow>.kinds[i].reads.query(W))` over the app
  role, served after the claim and before the body, so a step's reads see one snapshot and what they record describes
  one moment of the database; the body sees each name's rows as its record states them. Each row is recorded in
  `<P><Step><Name>Row(digest, …, root)`, keyed by the SHA-256 of its record's columns and its root, and the step's
  record of it is a reference, `<P><Step><Name>Read(step, ordinal, digest, root)`, contained in its row and in the
  step's result: a row two steps of one root found is stored once (an insert of a row already present is no change),
  so a chain of turns that reads a conversation as it grows stores each of its rows once, linear in the thread, where
  a copy per step was quadratic (DCAN's turns read their whole thread; metis's ask).
  - Named, because a decision reads several relations: the child's ledger and the nearest content.
  - One statement, because a step's record is its input: reads taken on several snapshots would record a moment that
    never was.
  - A grizzle query, because den's own read forms (equality rows, a nearest read from a stored anchor) could state
    neither a range, a set of values, an exact order nor a search from a vector the input carries (DCAN's catalog
    search, its route-scoped filters and its hybrid of vectors and full text), and a second query language in den
    would trail grizzle's; a function of the app role, because the flow is declared before the schema its fragment is
    spread into, which the role is over.
  - Recorded, never searched again: which rows an HNSW search returns depends on the index and the database owner's
    pgvector settings as well as the facts (grizzle's README), so the record is what the step saw. A record holds no
    vector, which the body never sees.
  - Rejected: a search the body runs itself, which would give a body the database (proposal, section 3.7); the rows as
    one array or JSON column, a blob; a record of only the found rows' keys into the app's relations, which a put or a
    delete there would rewrite and which records nothing a query computes; and a copy of every row per step.
- **A number is `Schema.Finite`, held finite by the database (2026-11-18).** Effect's `schemaNumber` diagnostic
  refuses `Schema.Number`, so a number field is `Schema.Finite`, lowered to double precision under a row check
  (`Num.isFinite`), so the database holds what the schema states.
- **A model's structured answer is relations (settled by the engine's need, 2026-11-18).** A step's input, output and
  error take, beside columns and Options, an array of columns or of structs of columns and a tagged union of structs
  of columns, as the proposal's section 6 planned. An array is an ordered family, `<Owner><Field>(owner, ordinal, …)`
  keyed by both, contained in its owner and each ordinal past 0 in its predecessor, so it is gap-free from 0 as a
  claim's attempts are. A tagged union is its tag, a roster column of its owner's row, and a relation per variant,
  `<Owner><Field><Tag>(owner, …)`, contained in the owner's rows of that tag and containing them, so a variant's row
  exists exactly where the tag names it; a missing one is a broken law, and reading it back dies. Every role still
  certifies READ COMMITTED. So a decision (what to serve, the items it weighed, how sure it is) is typed rows the app
  joins, committed with the step's result once, and read back whole by the scope's finish.
  - Rejected: a JSON or jsonb column of the answer, a blob; one wide relation of every variant's fields, nullable
    where a variant lacks them (no nullable column, proposal section 6); variant relations with no tag column, where
    "exactly one variant" would need a key across relations; an array column (`text[]`), which no law reads into and
    no query joins; unions and arrays nested past one struct, records and recursion, which no answer the engine asks
    for needs yet.

## Owner ruling, 2026-11-19: correctness first

- **Correct first, fast after (Bjorn, 2026-11-19).** "We need to do whatever we need for it to be theoretically
  correct, and performance only after correctness." grizzle judges a selection-sided containment whole, as a rule over
  a query, and keeps a plain containment into a declared key a foreign key; so den's own roles, its retirers and the
  app's committing role certify SERIALIZABLE, and den takes that. A call refused as `transient` changed nothing and
  runs again until it ends otherwise, with no bound; a transient is never an ending, a record or a retirement's
  outcome ([build/repin.md](build/repin.md), Certificates).

## Owner direction, 2026-11-20: AlphaTest is den's first user

- **AlphaTest's background work joins the workloads den serves (owner, 2026-11-20).** Bjorn: "we must implement what
  den is missing RIGHT NOW. Repin to the very latest green grizzle and implement these den features", and "den has
  zero backwards compat, zero legacy behavior. If we need something from den, push hard on the representation-first
  fix. Don't worry about breaking den, it has ZERO users right now. We would be its first user." This extends the
  workloads by AlphaTest's (the alphatest-cutover plan's background work): the Timeback writeback at grading, a chain
  per attempt on Timeback's seats; the gap analysis as a map and a reduce, one model call per wrong answer on
  Anthropic's seats and the cluster as its questions scope's finish, then the save, the stamp and the dispatch, and its
  plan (DCAN) as a chain of orchestrator turns with a fan-out scope of subagents; one time-spent delivery per segment;
  and the history load as parallel chains over an outside source. den-model holds each as a program under den's laws
  and its own (`metis.test.ts`).
- **What they needed is built, each a change of representation, never a branch.** The write that requests a root
  rings its admission once it has committed (`runtime/request.ts`); a turn rings what its end made eligible, the steps
  waiting on a seat it gave back among them; a finish answers an output, a child scope ends as its finish does, and an
  edge may leave a child scope kind, so the next stage after a scope is an ordinary edge spawned in its parent; and a
  body whose source named when to call again fails with `Later`, a wait that spends nothing, due the source's own delay.
- **A step goes again until its call settles, or never; no count bounds it (settled by metis's Timeback writes and
  Bjorn's ruling, 2026-11-21).** Bjorn: "hard delete ALL arbitrary retries", since "it should already be handled upstream
  by den retries": den's steps (and the transport's redelivery) are the only retry an app has, so an app keeps no retry
  loop, count, backoff or give-up cap of its own. A step kind and a finish state whether they go again after a turn that
  ended without a result, one representation (`Again`) for both: `retry`, after a spent attempt, each due a rung of
  den's backoff later, and `waits`, on their source, each a body that failed with `Later`, spending nothing and due at
  the instant the source named. `untilSettled` goes again as often as the step ends so, and only its result (an answer,
  or its typed error) ends it, so a write to an outside system is never dropped while the system answers "not now"
  (metis retries a Timeback write until Timeback settles it, and only a refusal ends it), the step joined by its scope
  throughout. `none` never goes again: the first such end fails the step, recorded as exhausted, so its scope's finish
  reads it failed (a call that must not run twice). A retry records why its attempt ended without a result, `spent`,
  `transient` or `later` (a roster where it was a flag of spent or not), so each time a step went again is a fact of
  the graph.
  - Rejected: a count of retries or waits (`attempts(n)`, `waits: n`), which every consumer filled with a number of its
    own choosing (upstream's DCAN's five waits and its hole-filling adapter's `maxAttempts: 3` are upstream's choices,
    not limits a source publishes), so it only invited indefensible numbers; a counted arm returns only beside a maximum
    a source publishes, with its evidence. Also a sentinel count (Infinity, -1) or an optional one meaning no bound; two
    shapes for the two (a ladder of attempts beside a count of waits), which spell one concept twice; a `Later` turned
    into a spent attempt that retries forever, which trades the source's named delay for den's rungs; a deadline
    measured from the step's first due, which a delay the source names can overshoot in one wait and which no source
    publishes; and a sleep or a counter inside the body, which would give a body a memory of its earlier attempts.
- **A call goes again past its transient refusals by den's `settled`, and grizzle keeps no retry (Bjorn,
  2026-11-21).** Bjorn: den is the only retry mechanism, with no retries anywhere in grizzle. So the combinator that
  runs a grizzle call again at once while it is refused as `transient`, and the failure it ends with (`Settled`, every
  CallError but `transient`), are den's (`runtime/settled.ts`, @bjornpagen/den/runtime/settled): every printed
  binding runs its calls through it, and an app runs its own calls to its served programs through it too. It keeps no
  count and no delay ([build/repin.md](build/repin.md), Certificates); a caller that answers by a deadline states it
  on its call (`Effect.timeout`).
  - Rejected: grizzle's `settled`, a retry inside the library under den; a copy in each app, a second spelling of one
    concept.
- **A schedule starts a root per member of its roster (settled by the plan's need, 2026-11-20).** `schedule(cron,
  members, (at, member) => input)` starts one root per member at each instant its cron names, keyed by the instant
  and the member, so a nightly copy of each of W1's relations or each of W3's collections is one schedule, not a root
  kind per member; a schedule of one root is a roster of one, the one form. Its trigger holds the roots an instant
  starts, `roster(at)`, each member with its root's input, so the census requests each by its key and nothing reads a
  member back.
  - Rejected: a single-root schedule beside a roster form, two forms for one thing; a key of the instant alone with
    the member in the input, which would collapse every member's root of an instant into one.
- **A step is joined on its input and the facts it awaits (settled by AlphaTest's need, 2026-11-20).** AlphaTest's gap
  analysis must not run before the gradebook holds its attempt's result, which another root's step delivers, and its
  dispatch reads what the front-run did (the cutover's review, findings 2 and 13); a declared read that retried while
  the fact was missing spent attempts and polled. So a step kind declares `awaits`, facts of the app's relations each
  named by its whole key, every key field held by an input field (`awaitFact(Delivered, { attempt: "review" })`), and is
  eligible only once every awaited row exists: the scheduler's snapshot lists the steps waiting on a fact, a turn of one
  waits (`awaiting`) with no claim and nothing spent, and the commit that writes an awaited relation, in any flow the
  app runs, rings the steps its rows made ready, as a spawn rings a step whose fact came first, so whichever commit
  completes the pair rings it; the census heals a ring that is lost, and no ring can be lost to both commits, since
  each reads after its own commit. An input field that holds an awaited key is typed as that key field, so the printed
  programs join the input to the fact.
  - Rejected: a feed edge with several parents among den's own nodes, which needs a correlation of parents den has no
    key for, where a node's write of an app fact is that key; a read that must find its row, which would join the
    step's eligibility to whatever a read can state; a cross-flow request from a commit, which would make the
    writeback own the gap analysis's start.


## Owner order, 2026-11-21: metis's essay grading

- **A finish's endings carry their children's inputs (settled by metis's need, 2026-11-21).** The essay grader's
  finish records a decline for each draw that failed, by the application the draw graded, which a failed ending did not
  name: an ending was `Done` with its output or `Failed` with nothing. So `Ending<I, O>` is `Done` with its input and
  output or `Failed` with its input, the finish's reads of its children's inputs set reads beside their outputs, each
  ending's input found by its step, which every child holds by law.
- **A read's record holds the rows its query's attachments deliver (settled by metis's need, 2026-11-21).** The essay
  grader reads an item's documents as pantry scans them, each document's nodes and each node's attributes and options,
  a query whose rows carry attachments to any depth, which a flat record could not record. So a record's field is a
  column, an `Option` of a record (an `"option"` attachment) or an array of records (an `"array"` one), and each
  attachment's rows are relations of their own, keyed by the read's row's digest and the places of the arrays above
  them, gap-free, contained in their owner; the row's digest covers every row under it, so a root stores a tree once and
  two trees that differ anywhere are two rows. An attachment's relation is named by the read and its field, numbered
  where a field name repeats, so a deep record's names stay within PostgreSQL's identifiers.
  - Rejected: an attachment's rows content-addressed one by one, which names a relation per path all the same and adds
    a relation of references per array.
- **A step commits through the app's own ChangeSet, in its commit's call (settled by metis's need, 2026-11-21).** The
  essay grader records its evaluation through the app's ChangeSet, which inserts what an insert of facts cannot state
  (its rows from parameters, its instants the database's), so a step's `writes` is `through(changes, clock)`, a
  ChangeSet `changes` makes of the flow's app role, or `writesNothing`; den's commit is
  `ChangeSet.union(<den's facts>, changes(W))`, clocked for den's spawns' `now` and each instant `clock` names, and the
  body returns its output and that ChangeSet's served input, its parameters and its array members' facts (an insert's
  rows `AdditionIn`, nested members under their owners). A refusal of either refuses both: the step ends failed by the
  law, with nothing of its own or of the app's committed. A commit rings the steps its rows made ready by what its
  outcome counts, whatever its ChangeSet holds.
  - den's commit names `steps`, `root` and `now` itself, and a ChangeSet's free parameters are one namespace, so
    `through` refuses at the type level a ChangeSet that names any of them: a served commit binds no value when it is
    built, so den cannot close its own parameters with `bind`. A scope of parameters a call still supplies is grizzle's
    to add.
  - Rejected: the fragment committed in a call of its own, which commits the step's result whether or not the app's
    write stands; a declaration of the relations a step writes, which states less than a ChangeSet and could disagree
    with it.
- **A step may be due at the instant its input holds (settled by metis's need, 2026-11-21).** A timed sitting closes at
  its own deadline, which is data, the limit times the extended time with the time left carried, so no delay a kind
  declares can state it (BUG-01-11, #3316). So a step kind's `due` is `atSpawn`, `after(millis)` or `dueAt((input) =>
  input.deadline)`, the instant an `instant` column of its input holds, den's `DenInstant` (a brand of that name is
  den's own contract). The instant is the database's: the write that requested the root computes it from its own reading
  of the database's clock (`Num.add(now, …)` cast to `DenInstant`), or a stored instant is carried, and no host mints one.
  A spawn writes such a step's due instant as given, beside the steps due a delay after the call's reading, which the
  statement adds, and rings it timed to its instant from a reading after the call, the census as the net.
  - Rejected: a polling chain that rechecks a deadline, which spends a turn per period and runs late by one; a delay
    computed on the host from its own clock to the deadline, which names an instant the database never read.
- **A turn runs only on the deployment its environment serves (settled by metis's need, 2026-11-21).** Vercel Queues
  partitions a topic by deployment, so a wake rung from a deployment lands on it again, and the census re-rings itself
  from wherever it ran: every deployment that ever rang kept its own census chain, and before a migration fenced it
  `stale`, a replaced deployment's old code ran beside the new one's. So an environment's serving deployment is a fact
  of each flow's coordination group, `<P>Served(environment, at, deployment)`, written by the deploy step's `promote`
  at the database's clock, the environment serving its latest; a host names where it runs once at its boundary (`Host`,
  Vercel's `VERCEL_ENV` and `VERCEL_DEPLOYMENT_ID`), and a turn's first reading, one clocked read, reads the deployment
  its environment serves beside the database's instant. A wake on a deployment its environment no longer serves ends
  `replaced`, and one in an environment no promotion named yet `unserved`: either writes nothing and rings nothing, so
  the replaced deployment's chains, its census's among them, end at their next ring, and the serving deployment's
  census heals what they left.
  - Rejected: a Vercel API call on a turn's path; the environment's variables alone, which name no replacement.
